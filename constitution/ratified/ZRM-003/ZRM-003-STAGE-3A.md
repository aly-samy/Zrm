# 1. Constitutional Purpose

Stage 3A establishes the constitutional process for identifying candidate mathematical truths that emerge from the frozen Constitutional Axioms established in Stage 2.

Discovery precedes proof.

Hypothesis precedes theorem.

Stage 3A generates hypotheses.

Stage 3A does not establish mathematical truth.

The purpose of Stage 3A is to map the mathematical landscape before any theorem is formally proven.

Accordingly:

- No theorem is accepted.
- No theorem is proven.
- No theorem is ratified.
- No theorem is frozen.

Stage 3A defines **how discovery occurs**, not **what has been discovered**.

---

# 2. Constitutional Question

Stage 3A answers exactly one constitutional question:

> **What mathematical truths appear to emerge from the Constitutional Axioms?**

Stage 3A does **not** answer:

- Are these truths proven?
- Are these truths necessary?
- Are these truths complete?
- Are these truths correct?

Those determinations belong exclusively to Stage 3B.

---

# 3. Constitutional Authority

Stage 3A derives authority exclusively from:

- Stage 0 — Constitutional Benchmark Suite
- Stage 1 — Constitutional Vocabulary
- Stage 2 — Frozen Constitutional Axioms

Stage 3A SHALL NOT introduce:

- new axioms;
- new vocabulary;
- implementation assumptions;
- technology-specific behavior;
- business semantics.

---

# 4. Scope

Stage 3A SHALL define:

- Discovery methodology
- Candidate theorem template
- Discovery principles
- Discovery gates
- Candidate Registry governance
- Transition requirements to Stage 3B

Stage 3A SHALL NOT:

- prove theorems;
- ratify theorems;
- freeze theorems;
- define implementations;
- define algorithms;
- introduce new vocabulary;
- introduce new axioms;
- determine theorem validity;
- determine theorem completeness;
- determine theorem dependencies;
- determine theorem necessity.

Those determinations belong exclusively to Stage 3B.

# 5. Discovery Methodology

## Step 1 — Select Constitutional Axioms

Begin from one or more frozen Constitutional Axioms.

Examples:

- Identity
- Event
- Time
- Graph
- Projection
- Hierarchy

---

## Step 2 — Hypothesize Candidate Mathematical Consequences

Explore potential mathematical consequences.

This step generates hypotheses only.

Nothing discovered during Stage 3A constitutes mathematical proof.

---

## Step 3 — Explore Cross-Axiom Interactions

Investigate possible relationships between multiple axioms.

Examples include:

- Identity + Time
- Event + Projection
- Graph + Hierarchy
- Event + Identity

Cross-axiom interactions often reveal candidate mathematical truths.

---

## Step 4 — Search Constitutional Witnesses

Search the Stage 0 Constitutional Benchmark Suite.

Examples include:

- GS1 / Digital Product Passport
- Double-Entry Accounting
- Git
- HL7 FHIR
- CAD / BIM
- DNS / BGP
- Knowledge Graphs
- Blockchain
- AI Memory Systems
- Interstellar Civilization
- Natural Sciences

Candidate truths appearing across multiple independent witnesses are more likely to represent constitutional mathematics.

---

## Step 5 — Identify Recurring Mathematical Truths

Evaluate whether the candidate:

- appears across multiple witnesses;
- is independent of implementation;
- can be expressed using Constitutional Vocabulary;
- represents a reusable mathematical relationship.

---

## Step 6 — Draft Candidate Theorem

Record the hypothesis using the Constitutional Candidate Theorem Template.

---

## Step 7 — Apply Discovery Gates

Every candidate theorem SHALL pass the Discovery Gates.

Candidates that pass enter the Candidate Registry.

Candidates that fail return to discovery for refinement.

Discovery is iterative.

---

## Step 8 — Update Candidate Registry

The Candidate Registry may evolve by:

- adding candidates;
- revising candidates;
- merging candidates;
- splitting candidates;
- discarding candidates.

The Registry remains mutable throughout Stage 3A.

---

# 6. Candidate Theorem Template

Every Candidate Theorem SHALL contain:

| Field | Description |
|--------|-------------|
| ID | Temporary identifier (TH-XXX) |
| Name | Canonical theorem name |
| Statement | Mathematical claim |
| Intuition | Why it appears true |
| Related Witnesses | Supporting Constitutional Witnesses |
| Hypothesized Dependencies | Candidate supporting axioms |
| Confidence | Low / Medium / High |
| Discovery Rationale | How the candidate was discovered |
| Status | Registry lifecycle state |

# 7. Discovery Principles

## DP-1 — Minimality

Prefer the smallest independent theorem.

---

## DP-2 — Reusability

Prefer mathematical truths appearing across multiple domains.

---

## DP-3 — Implementation Independence

Discovery SHALL remain implementation-agnostic.

---

## DP-4 — Optimization Independence

Performance considerations SHALL NOT influence theorem discovery.

---

## DP-5 — Atomicity

One theorem SHALL express one mathematical truth.

---

## DP-6 — Discovery Does Not Imply Correctness

Every discovered theorem remains a hypothesis until formally proven.

---

## DP-7 — Discovery Does Not Imply Necessity

A discovered theorem may later prove unnecessary or derivable from another theorem.

---

## DP-8 — Evolutionary Discovery

Candidates may be revised, merged, split, or discarded throughout discovery.

---

# 8. Discovery Gates

Every candidate theorem SHALL satisfy the following Discovery Gates.

| Gate | Question |
|------|----------|
| DG-1 | Novelty — Is this genuinely new? |
| DG-2 | Non-Axiom — Is it distinct from existing axioms? |
| DG-3 | Non-Definition — Is it more than a definition? |
| DG-4 | Non-Implementation — Does it avoid implementation assumptions? |
| DG-5 | Non-Optimization — Does it avoid performance assumptions? |
| DG-6 | Non-Corollary — Is it more than a trivial consequence of one axiom? |
| DG-7 | Multi-Witness — Does it appear across independent Constitutional Witnesses unless explicitly justified? |
| DG-8 | Mathematical Significance — Does it express a meaningful mathematical relationship? |
| DG-9 | Constitutional Necessity — Would its absence materially reduce the expressive power, completeness, or coherence of the constitutional mathematics? |

Failure of any Discovery Gate does not permanently reject a theorem.

Candidates failing a gate return to discovery for refinement.

# 10. Candidate Registry Governance

Stage 3A SHALL produce a separate living artifact:

- is a working artifact;

The Constitution governs discovery.

---

# 11. Registry Lifecycle

Every Candidate Theorem SHALL progress through the following lifecycle.

```text
Hypothesis
      │
      ▼
Discovery Review
      │
      ▼
Discovery Gates Passed
      │
      ▼
Proof Eligible
      │
      ▼
Under Proof (Stage 3B)
      │
      ▼
Ratified
      │
      ▼
Frozen
```

No theorem may bypass this lifecycle.

---

# 12. Constitutional Prohibitions

Stage 3A SHALL NOT determine:

- theorem validity;
- theorem completeness;
- theorem dependencies;
- theorem necessity.

Those determinations belong exclusively to Stage 3B.

---

# 13. Exit Criteria

Stage 3A is complete only when:

- successive discovery rounds produce no materially new candidate theorems;
- every retained candidate has passed the Discovery Gates;
- every retained candidate has been recorded within the Candidate Registry;
- the Council determines that further discovery is unlikely to reveal constitutionally significant mathematical truths;
- the Candidate Registry is declared complete.

Stage 3A concludes discovery only.

Proof remains outside its authority.

---

# 14. Stage 3B Eligibility

A Candidate Theorem SHALL be eligible for Stage 3B only if:

1. it has passed all Discovery Gates;
2. it has been entered into the Candidate Registry;
3. its Registry Status is **Proof Eligible**;
4. the Council has explicitly approved it for proof;
5. no unresolved discovery objections remain.

Only Proof Eligible candidates may enter Stage 3B.

---

# 15. Transition to Stage 3B

Once the Exit Criteria have been satisfied, control passes to Stage 3B.

```text
Stage 3A

        ▼

Proof Eligible Candidates
        │
        ▼
Stage 3B
Formal Proof
        │
        ▼
Ratification
        │
        ▼
Frozen Theorems
```
