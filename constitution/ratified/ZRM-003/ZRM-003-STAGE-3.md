# 1. Purpose

Stage 3 establishes the constitutional framework for discovering, proving, validating, and ratifying mathematical theorems that emerge from the Constitutional Axioms.

A theorem is **never accepted by declaration**.

A theorem becomes constitutional only after successful formal derivation, adversarial review, benchmark validation, and ratification.

Stage 3 introduces **no new primitives** and **no new axioms**.

Its sole responsibility is to derive unavoidable truths from the already frozen constitutional foundation.

---

# 2. Constitutional Question

Stage 3 answers exactly one constitutional question:

> **Which mathematical truths necessarily follow from the Constitutional Axioms?**

It does not answer:

- how systems are implemented
- how algorithms operate
- how software executes
- how infrastructure is deployed

Those belong to lower constitutional layers.

---

# 3. Constitutional Authority

Stage 3 derives authority exclusively from:

- Stage 0 — Constitutional Witnesses
- Stage 1 — Constitutional Vocabulary
- Stage 2 — Constitutional Axioms

No theorem may derive authority from:

- implementation
- technology
- engineering preference
- optimization
- software architecture

---

# 4. Stage Structure

Stage 3 consists of two constitutional phases.

---

## Stage 3A — Constitutional Theorem Discovery

Purpose:

Discover candidate theorems that appear to follow from the frozen axioms.

Outputs:

- Candidate Theorem Registry
- Candidate theorem statements
- Expected constitutional witnesses
- **Hypothesized** dependency sets
- Initial review history

Stage 3A is exploratory.

Nothing produced here possesses constitutional authority.

No dependency graph is considered final.

---

## Stage 3B — Constitutional Theorem Ratification

Purpose:

Transform candidate theorems into constitutional mathematics.

Activities include:

- formal derivation
- dependency discovery
- proof verification
- benchmark validation
- adversarial review
- constitutional ratification

Outputs:

- Ratified Constitutional Theorem Registry
- Frozen theorem proofs
- Final dependency graph

Only Stage 3B may freeze a theorem.

---

# 5. Dependency Discovery Principle

The complete dependency graph of a theorem SHALL NOT be considered authoritative until completion of the formal proof.

Dependencies identified during Stage 3A are hypotheses only.

Formal proof construction is the constitutional process that discovers the complete dependency graph.

The dependency graph becomes frozen only after successful theorem ratification.

---

# 6. Constitutional Theorem Requirements

Every constitutional theorem SHALL satisfy every requirement.

---

## TR-1 — Formal Derivation

Every theorem SHALL be formally derivable from:

- Constitutional Axioms
- previously ratified Constitutional Theorems

No external assumptions are permitted.

---

## TR-2 — Dependency Transparency

Every theorem SHALL explicitly identify every constitutional dependency discovered during formal proof.

---

## TR-3 — Non-Circularity

No theorem may directly or indirectly depend upon itself.

Dependency graphs SHALL be acyclic.

---

## TR-4 — Vocabulary Compliance

Every theorem SHALL use only Stage 1 constitutional vocabulary.

---

## TR-5 — Technology Independence

No theorem may assume:

- databases
- software
- hardware
- programming languages
- communication protocols

---

## TR-6 — Implementation Independence

No theorem may describe:

- execution
- optimization
- deployment
- runtime behavior

---

## TR-7 — Universality

Every theorem SHALL hold universally within the constitutional domain defined by the Stage 2 axioms.

Its truth is bounded only by those axioms and is independent of implementation, technology, observer, or execution environment.

---

## TR-8 — Benchmark Validation

Every theorem SHALL successfully model every applicable Constitutional Witness.

Failure against any applicable witness constitutes constitutional evidence requiring review.

---

## TR-9 — Failure Robustness

Every theorem SHALL survive adversarial counterexamples drawn from multiple constitutional witness domains.

Examples include:

- Blockchain
- Git
- Biology
- Banking
- GS1
- Healthcare
- Interstellar
- Artificial Intelligence

# 12. Validation by Construction

Passing constitutional review does not immediately produce Final Freeze.

The theorem enters Candidate status and proceeds through construction validation.

Validation includes:

- proof construction
- benchmark execution
- cross-domain interaction
- dependency verification
- adversarial testing
- consistency with previously ratified theorems

Construction exists to validate the sufficiency of the constitutional foundation.

---

# 13. Constitutional Failure Interpretation

If a theorem fails **Formal Derivation (TR-1)**:

The theorem is rejected.

---

If a theorem passes Formal Derivation but fails:

- Benchmark Validation (TR-8), or
- Failure Robustness (TR-9),

the failure SHALL NOT be interpreted as proof that the theorem is incorrect.

Instead:

the failure constitutes constitutional evidence that the Stage 2 Axiom Registry is incomplete or incorrect.

Stage 2 SHALL reopen through constitutional amendment procedures.

The theorem remains unratified until Stage 2 is resolved.

---

# 14. Exit Criteria

A theorem may receive Final Freeze only when:

- all review gates pass
- proof is complete
- dependency graph is finalized
- benchmark validation succeeds
- adversarial review succeeds
- construction validation succeeds
- no constitutional conflicts remain
