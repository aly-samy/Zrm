# Purpose

This document establishes the constitutional framework for formally proving, validating, classifying, and ratifying mathematical theorems discovered during Stage 3A.

Stage 3B transforms discovered candidate theorems into constitutional authority.

Stage 3B performs **proof**, not **discovery**.

No new mathematical discoveries, axioms, vocabulary, or constitutional principles may be introduced during this stage.

The framework defined by this document is itself constitutional authority.

The results produced by applying this framework become constitutional authority only after successful ratification.

# Proof Methodology

Every candidate theorem SHALL pass through six constitutional phases.

---

## Phase 1 — Dependency Analysis

Determine every constitutional dependency.

Questions:

- Which axioms are required?
- Which vocabulary is required?
- Which previously ratified theorems are required?
- Are all dependencies already constitutional authority?

Deliverable:

**Dependency Graph**

---

## Phase 2 — Proof Construction

Construct the shortest complete proof.

Proof SHALL contain only constitutional authority.

Allowed:

- axioms
- definitions
- previously ratified theorems

Forbidden:

- intuition
- implementation
- examples as proof
- hidden assumptions

Deliverable:

**Formal Proof**

---

## Phase 3 — Independence Analysis

Determine whether the theorem contributes new constitutional knowledge.

Questions:

- Can it already be derived?
- Is it implied by existing authority?
- Does removal reduce constitutional power?

Possible outcomes:

- Independent
- Corollary
- Duplicate

Deliverable:

**Independence Report**

---

## Phase 4 — Counterexample Search

The Council SHALL intentionally attempt to invalidate the theorem.

Required attack categories include:

- logical contradiction
- benchmark witnesses
- mathematical models
- edge cases
- graph reasoning
- distributed systems
- identity mutation
- temporal reasoning
- adversarial construction

Rule:

A single successful counterexample invalidates the theorem.

Deliverable:

**Counterexample Report**

---

## Phase 5 — Minimality Review

Determine whether the theorem is constitutionally minimal.

Questions:

- Can anything be removed?
- Can it be simplified?
- Can two theorems merge?
- Should one theorem split?
- Is the theorem irreducible?

Deliverable:

**Minimality Report**

---

## Phase 6 — Ratification Vote

Every candidate SHALL receive exactly one constitutional classification.

Deliverable:

- Final Classification
- Constitutional Vote

## PG-1 — Formal Proof Exists

---

## PG-2 — Constitutional Authority Only

The proof SHALL rely exclusively upon:

- constitutional vocabulary
- constitutional axioms
- previously ratified theorems

---

## PG-3 — No Circular Reasoning

---

## PG-4 — No Hidden Assumptions

All dependencies SHALL be explicit.

No unstated assumptions are permitted.

---

## PG-5 — Independence Classification

The theorem SHALL be classified as either:

- Independent
- Corollary
- Duplicate

---

## PG-6 — No Successful Counterexample

Every documented adversarial attack SHALL fail.

---

## PG-7 — Proof Soundness

Every logical deduction SHALL be mathematically valid and fully justified.

No invalid inference is permitted.

---

## PG-8 — Universal Validity

The theorem SHALL hold throughout its declared constitutional domain.

---

## PG-9 — Adds Constitutional Power

Removing the theorem SHALL materially reduce constitutional expressive power.

---

## PG-10 — Strict Minimality Confirmation

The Council SHALL confirm:

- the theorem is irreducible
- the theorem is necessary
- no existing theorem replaces it
- no existing axiom replaces it

# Dependency Ordering

The following ordering governs theorem proof.

---

## DO-1 — Axiom-Only Theorems

Theorems depending solely upon constitutional axioms SHALL be proven first.

---

## DO-2 — Dependent Theorems

Theorems depending upon unratified mathematical families SHALL be deferred until those families complete Stage 3A and Stage 3B.

---

## DO-3 — No Premature Proof

No theorem SHALL be proven before all constitutional dependencies are ratified.

---

## DO-4 — Cross-Domain Isolation

A theorem SHALL NOT introduce variables belonging to an unratified mathematical family.

If such variables are required, the theorem SHALL be deferred until that family becomes constitutional authority.

This preserves mathematical isolation and prevents category leakage.

# Final Classifications

Every candidate SHALL receive exactly one final classification.

| Classification | Meaning | Registry |
|---------------|---------|----------|
| Ratified Theorem | Proof succeeds; constitutionally independent | Enter Registry |
| Corollary | Mathematically valid but derivable | Do Not Enter |
| Duplicate | Already represented elsewhere | Do Not Enter |
| Invalid | Proof fails or counterexample succeeds | Removed |
| Deferred | Depends upon unresolved constitutional authority | Hold Until Dependencies Complete |

No theorem may receive multiple classifications.
