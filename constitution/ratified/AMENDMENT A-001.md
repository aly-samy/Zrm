## 1. PURPOSE

This amendment formally resolves the constitutional debate regarding the status of CL-13 Temporal within the Zyppi Reality Graph.

During the `WS-03A.0` Constitutional Cluster Map Roundtable, a motion was introduced proposing the removal of CL-13 Temporal from the constitutional cluster registry and its reclassification as a graph-level property rather than a constitutional cluster.

Following council review, the motion did not achieve constitutional consensus and was rejected.

This amendment records the rationale, ruling, and constitutional implications of that decision.

---

## 2. ORIGINAL MOTION

### Proposed Motion:

> «Remove CL-13 Temporal from the Constitutional Cluster Registry and treat time exclusively as graph metadata and infrastructure-level indexing.»

**Primary rationale presented:**

- Time is a fundamental property rather than a constitutional entity.
- Temporal queries can be executed through bitemporal indexing.
- Eliminating CL-13 would reduce graph complexity.
- Temporal concepts could be represented through metadata rather than owned entities.

---

## 3. COUNCIL REVIEW

The council determined that the proposal incorrectly conflated:

1. Temporal storage mechanics
2. Temporal governance concepts

The existence of timestamps at the infrastructure level does not eliminate the need for constitutional ownership of temporal concepts.

The council further determined that:

- Time as metadata and
- Temporal governance as ontology

are separate concerns.

---

## 4. CONSTITUTIONAL FINDINGS

The council unanimously recognized that multiple constitutional entities require explicit temporal ownership, including but not limited to:

- Timestamp
- Duration
- Validity Window
- Effective Period
- Expiration Period
- Temporal Context
- Lifecycle State Definition
- Business Period
- Academic Term
- Fiscal Period
- Regulatory Effective Date
- Compliance Window
- Contract Validity Range

These concepts are not merely metadata.

They possess governance rules, relationships, validation requirements, and independent semantic meaning.

---

## 5. OWNERSHIP ANALYSIS

The council evaluated alternative ownership locations.

### **CL-07 Contract**

> Rejected.

**Reason:**

Contracts consume temporal concepts but do not own them.

---

### **CL-14 Compliance**

> Rejected.

**Reason:**

Compliance frameworks reference temporal concepts but do not own them.

---

### **CL-15 Schema**

> Rejected.

**Reason:**

Schema describes temporal concepts but does not own them.

---

### **CL-17 Graph Core**

> Rejected.

**Reason:**

Graph Core owns infrastructure and indexing mechanisms.

Graph Core does not own business semantics.

---

### Independent Cluster Ownership

> Accepted.

**Reason:**

Temporal concepts possess distinct governance, lifecycle rules, and cross-cluster applicability.

Therefore they require independent constitutional ownership.

---

## 6. CONSTITUTIONAL RULING

**The council hereby rules:**

- CL-13 Temporal SHALL remain a constitutional cluster.

- Temporal concepts SHALL possess constitutional ownership under CL-13.

- Temporal concepts MAY be implemented through infrastructure indexing, database optimization, bitemporal storage, or graph metadata.

- Implementation strategy SHALL NOT alter constitutional ownership.

---

## 7. CLARIFICATION OF RESPONSIBILITIES

### CL-13 Temporal Owns

- Temporal primitives
- Temporal governance concepts
- Validity constructs
- Duration constructs
- Temporal classifications
- Temporal contexts

**Examples:**

- Timestamp
- Duration
- ValidityWindow
- EffectivePeriod

- AcademicTerm

---

### CL-17 Graph Core Owns

- Temporal indexing
- Graph snapshots
- Historical graph views
- Bitemporal storage mechanisms
- Time-travel query infrastructure

**Examples:**

- Snapshot Registry
- Temporal Index
- Historical Query Engine

---

## 8. CONSTITUTIONAL PRINCIPLE

### TP-001 — Temporal Ownership Principle

> «Temporal governance concepts SHALL be owned by CL-13 Temporal.

> Temporal storage, indexing, and retrieval mechanisms SHALL be owned by CL-17 Graph Core.

> Semantic ownership and implementation ownership are distinct constitutional concerns.»
