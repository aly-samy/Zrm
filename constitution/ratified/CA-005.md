# CA-005 — Trust Registry Constitutional Definition
---|---
Status:| Ratified Constitutional Amendment

# 1. Purpose
This amendment establishes the constitutional definition, ownership, governance, and operational role of the Trust Registry.

The Trust Registry exists to provide authoritative validation of authority claims before Contracts, Transactions, Relationships, and Authority Anchors become active.

This amendment introduces no new cluster.

This amendment introduces no new constitutional primitive.

# 2. Constitutional Problem
Multiple constitutional documents require Trust validation.

**Examples include:**
- WS-03A.5 CP-CTR-08
> "Authority claims SHALL be validated through the Trust Registry."
- WS-03A.6 CD-TXN-07
> "Sovereign Authorities SHALL be validated before Transaction commitment."
- WS-03C RC-008
> "Authority requirements SHALL be validated by the Trust Registry before relationship activation."

Prior to this amendment, the Trust Registry was referenced but undefined.

This amendment resolves that gap.

# 3. Constitutional Definition
## Trust Registry
### Definition:
A constitutional validation service that determines whether an authority claim can be considered valid, active, and executable within the ZRM.

The Trust Registry does not create authority.

The Trust Registry validates authority.

Authority originates elsewhere.

Trust determines whether that authority may be exercised.

# 4. Ownership
## Registry Owner:
## CL-17 Graph Core
### Reason:
Trust validation operates across all clusters.

No single business cluster owns authority verification.

The Trust Registry functions as cross-cluster governance infrastructure.

# 5. Constitutional Scope
The Trust Registry SHALL validate:
- Authority Anchors
- Delegated Authority
- Sovereign Authority
- Regulatory Authority
- Organizational Authority
- System Authority
- External Authority Claims

The Trust Registry SHALL NOT:
- Create authority
- Modify authority
- Transfer authority
- Revoke authority

Those functions remain owned by their originating constitutional structures.

# 6. Trust Assertion Model
The Trust Registry operates through Trust Assertions.

A Trust Assertion records that a specific authority claim has been evaluated.

**Each Trust Assertion SHALL contain:**
````
- assertion_id
- authority_subject
- authority_source
- validation_basis
- trust_status
- effective_from
- effective_to
- validator
- evidence_reference
````

# 7. Trust Status Lifecycle
A Trust Assertion SHALL exist in one of four states.

## VALID
Authority may be exercised.

## SUSPENDED
Authority temporarily unavailable.
No new execution may begin.

## REVOKED
Authority permanently invalid.
Execution prohibited.

## EXPIRED
Authority validity window ended.
Execution prohibited.

# 8. Validation Sources
Trust validation may be derived from:
- Government Registries
- Corporate Registries
- Compliance Registries
- Authority Anchors
- Legal Agreements
- Intent Contracts
- Cryptographic Proofs
- Approved Verification Systems

The Trust Registry does not prescribe verification methods.

It records validation outcomes.

# 9. Authority Validation Rule
**Before activation:**
- Authority Anchor
- Role Assignment
- Intent Contract
- Relationship Requiring Authority
- Transaction Requiring Authority
must possess at least one VALID Trust Assertion.

Failure to satisfy this requirement SHALL prevent activation.

# 10. Revocation Interaction
Trust Registry and Authority Revocation are distinct mechanisms.

CA-004 governs authority lifecycle.

CA-005 governs authority validation.

When an Authority Anchor enters REVOKED state:
- All associated Trust Assertions SHALL automatically enter REVOKED state.

# 11. Evidence Integration
Trust Assertions SHALL reference supporting evidence.

Evidence ownership remains unchanged.

**Evidence remains attached to:**
- Transactions
- Relationships
- External Validation Sources

The Trust Registry stores references only.

The Trust Registry never owns Evidence.

# 12. Relationship to CL-14 Compliance
CL-14 Compliance defines:
- regulatory rules
- obligations
- legal frameworks

Trust Registry determines whether a claimed authority satisfies those requirements.

Compliance defines requirements.

Trust validates eligibility.

# 13. Relationship to CL-16 Intelligence
Trust is not Intelligence.

Trust validation outcomes may generate Intelligence.

The resulting analysis belongs to CL-16.

The validation record belongs to the Trust Registry.

# 14. Constitutional Principles
## TP-001
Trust validates authority. Trust does not create authority.
## TP-002
Trust is cross-cluster infrastructure.
## TP-003
Trust Assertions are immutable.
Changes require supersession.
## TP-004
Trust Registry stores validation outcomes. It does not store authority itself.
## TP-005
Trust Registry references evidence. It never owns evidence.
## TP-006
Execution requiring authority SHALL fail when no VALID Trust Assertion exists.

The Trust Registry is hereby established as a CL-17 Graph Core governance subsystem responsible for validating authority claims across the Zyppi Reality Model.

This constitutional gap is closed.
