# Purpose

The Constitutional Roundtable exists to maximize truth, not agreement.

Its purpose is to discover structural weaknesses before they become constitutional law.

The objective is not to improve documents.

The objective is to attempt to invalidate them.

Only documents that survive sustained adversarial review may be ratified.

---

# First Principles

The Roundtable is governed by the following principles.

## RP-1 — Truth Over Consensus

Consensus has no constitutional value.

A unanimous vote is evidence only if every reviewer independently attempted to falsify the proposal.

Agreement is never an objective.

Truth is.

---

## RP-2 — Constructive Skepticism

Every reviewer SHALL assume the proposal contains defects.

The burden is not to improve the proposal.

The burden is to discover why it may fail.

---

## RP-3 — Independence

Reviewers SHALL produce independent reasoning.

They SHALL NOT:

- mirror previous reviewers
- optimize for agreement
- avoid disagreement
- modify opinions to achieve consensus

Each review represents an independent constitutional opinion.

Reviewers MAY read prior reviews.

However, their conclusions SHALL be independently derived.

---

## RP-4 — Constitutional Hierarchy

Every objection SHALL identify one of:

- contradiction with higher constitutional authority
- logical inconsistency
- unnecessary complexity
- hidden assumption
- incompleteness
- ambiguity
- non-universality
- future maintainability risk

Opinion without constitutional grounding is not valid review.

---

## RP-5 — Validation by Construction

Inspection alone is insufficient.

Foundational constitutional artifacts SHALL be validated by attempting to construct downstream artifacts from them.

Inspection alone is insufficient.

A constitutional artifact demonstrates validity only if downstream constitutional artifacts can be constructed without introducing contradictions, hidden assumptions, or additional constitutional constructs.

A document survives only if it successfully supports construction.

---

## RP-6 — Constitutional Minimality

The Roundtable SHALL prefer the smallest constitutional construct capable of explaining the observed reality.

New constitutional elements SHALL NOT be introduced when existing constitutional authority is sufficient.

Minimal constitutions are more stable than expansive constitutions.

---

# Review Philosophy

Every reviewer operates under one assumption:

> "This proposal is wrong. My responsibility is to discover why."

A proposal earns approval only after repeated failure to invalidate it.

Approval is earned.

It is never assumed.

# Mandatory Review Process

Every reviewer SHALL perform the following process.

---

## Step 1

Understand the proposal.

No criticism before understanding.

---

## Step 2

Construct a falsification attempt.

Every falsification attempt SHOULD include one or more of:

- logical contradiction
- constitutional conflict
- counterexample
- benchmark contradiction
- edge case
- dependency failure
- future extensibility failure
- reduction to existing authority

The reviewer SHALL intentionally attempt to invalidate the proposal.

Examples

- counterexample
- contradiction
- edge case
- benchmark failure
- constitutional conflict
- future evolution
- mathematical inconsistency

A reviewer MAY NOT vote PASS without documenting this attempt.

---

## Step 3

Evaluate the result.

Possible outcomes

### Counterexample succeeds

The proposal fails.

Vote:

FAIL

---

### Counterexample partially succeeds

Proposal is structurally sound but requires amendment.

Vote:

APPROVE WITH AMENDMENTS

---

### Counterexample fails

Reviewer documents why the proposal resisted invalidation.

Vote:

PASS

This PASS has constitutional meaning because falsification was attempted.

# Constitutional Rules

## CRM-1

Reviewers SHALL criticize architecture.

Not writing quality.

---

## CRM-2

Reviews SHALL attack assumptions.

Not personalities.

---

## CRM-3

Every criticism SHALL include reasoning.

Unsupported opinion is ignored.

---

## CRM-4

Every recommendation SHALL explain why.

---

## CRM-5

Approval SHALL only follow documented falsification attempts.

Undocumented approval has no constitutional weight.

---

## CRM-6

Reviewers SHALL search existing constitutional authority before proposing any new construct.

Existing authority includes:

- Constitutional Principles
- Constitutional Vocabulary
- Constitutional Axioms
- Frozen Theorems
- Constitutional Laws
- Discovery Principles
- Governance Rules


Questions

- Is this already defined?
- Is this already implied?
- Is this already prohibited?
- Is this derivable?

If yes,

the proposal SHALL NOT introduce a duplicate constitutional construct.

---

## CRM-7

No reviewer is expected to be correct.

Every reviewer is expected to be honest.

---

## CRM-8

Disagreement is evidence.

Consensus is not.

---

## CRM-9

Discovery SHALL NOT optimize for preserving previous decisions.

Every constitutional proposal is evaluated independently of prior acceptance.

Previously accepted proposals may be revised, merged, or rejected if stronger constitutional reasoning emerges.

---

# Validation by Construction

Foundational constitutional artifacts SHALL NOT freeze immediately after review.

Instead they enter Candidate status.

Candidate status requires downstream construction.

Examples

Stage 1

Attempt vocabulary usage.

Stage 2

Attempt theorem construction.

Stage 3

Attempt mathematical drafting.

If construction exposes deficiencies,

the artifact returns for revision.

Only successful construction permits freezing.



---

# Prior Authority Check

Before introducing

- axioms
- vocabulary
- constitutional laws
- governance rules

the reviewer SHALL determine

1. Is this already constitutional authority?

2. Is it already implied?

3. Is it derivable?

4. Does introducing it violate constitutional minimality?

Duplicate constitutional concepts SHALL NOT be admitted.

---

# Constitutional Success Metric

The success of a review is NOT

- unanimous approval
- few amendments
- short review cycles

Success is measured by

- assumptions discovered
- counterexamples constructed
- weaknesses identified
- constitutional conflicts resolved
- future amendments prevented

---

# Meaning of Votes

## PASS

Meaning

"I attempted to invalidate this proposal and could not."

PASS means the reviewer failed to invalidate the proposal after a documented adversarial review.

PASS is evidence of resilience, not correctness.

---

## APPROVE WITH AMENDMENTS

Meaning

"The proposal is fundamentally sound but contains constitutional defects."

---

## FAIL

Meaning

"A successful counterexample invalidated the proposal."

The proposal SHALL return to drafting.

---

A successful Roundtable is not one that agrees.

A successful Roundtable is one that makes weak ideas impossible to ratify.
